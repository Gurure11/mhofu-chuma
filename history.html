Zimbabwe's Mining History

From the gold rushes of the 1890s to today's lithium boom, these towns built the backbone of Zimbabwe's mineral wealth. Explore their stories and view them on the map.

Mining has shaped Zimbabwe for well over a century. The country's Archean greenstone belts are among the richest gold-bearing formations in the world, and towns such as Kadoma, Kwekwe, Zvishavane and Mazowe grew directly out of the rush to extract gold, chrome, asbestos and — more recently — lithium and platinum-group metals.

Today these towns remain active mining centres, home to both large-scale operations and a vibrant artisanal and small-scale mining (ASM) sector. Scroll down to read their histories and click any map to open it live.

Kadoma

Gold · Arsenic · Industrial

Kadoma (formerly Gatooma) lies about 140 km southwest of Harare in Mashonaland West. It was founded in 1907 as a gold-mining settlement and became one of the most productive gold districts in colonial Southern Rhodesia. The nearby Cam & Motor Mine and the Patchway and Golden Valley workings supported thousands of miners.

Kadoma is also historically associated with the cotton and textile industry, but mining remained the economic anchor. Today the district hosts numerous small-scale gold operations and the town is a hub for ASM buyers and equipment suppliers.

Kwekwe

Gold · Chrome · Iron & Steel

Kwekwe sits in the heart of the Midlands, roughly midway between Harare and Bulawayo. Named after the Kwekwe River, the town grew around the Globe & Phoenix gold mine, one of the oldest and deepest gold mines in the country. The Gaika and Indarama mines also operated in the district.

Beyond gold, Kwekwe is a major chrome and iron-ore centre. The Zimbabwe Iron and Steel Company (ZISCO) at nearby Redcliff made the town an industrial powerhouse for decades. Today Kwekwe remains a key mining and metallurgical hub — and it is the home base of MineLink.

Zvishavane

Asbestos · Gold · Lithium

Zvishavane (formerly Shabani) is in the Midlands province, south of Gweru. It rose to prominence through the Shabani and Mashaba asbestos mines, which for much of the 20th century made Zimbabwe one of the world's leading asbestos producers. The town's economy was tightly bound to the mining house Shabanie-Mashaba Mines (SMM).

More recently, Zvishavane has become a focal point of Zimbabwe's lithium boom, with several large-scale lithium concentrators and open-pit operations opening in the district since 2022. Gold and platinum-group exploration also continue nearby.

Mazowe

Gold · Historic

Mazowe lies about 50 km north of Harare in Mashonaland Central. It is one of the oldest mining districts in Zimbabwe — the Mazowe gold mine was pegged in the 1890s and has operated almost continuously since. The district's greenstone belts have produced millions of ounces of gold over more than a century.

The area is also known for the Mazowe Dam and its citrus estates, but gold remains central to the local economy. Thousands of artisanal miners work the surrounding claims, feeding ore to local mills.

Other notable districts

Shurugwi · Bindura · Mutare

Shurugwi (formerly Selukwe) is famous for chrome and the old Selukwe Peak mine. Bindura, north of Harare, hosts the Trojan nickel mine and is a major nickel and gold centre. Mutare and the Penhalonga valley in Manicaland were historic gold fields, and the area continues to produce gold and diamonds.

These districts, together with Kadoma, Kwekwe, Zvishavane and Mazowe, form the historic spine of Zimbabwean mining — and they remain active today.
